Customer interest in figures

A practical guide for the product, pricing, compliance and claims teams of insurers and MGAs. What questions do supervisors ask about customer interest, and how do you support the answers with market data?

1. Summary

Putting the customer's interest first is not a new concept for insurers. What is new is that supervisors increasingly ask you to demonstrate it with data: does the product fit the target market, do loyal customers pay a fair premium, are claims handled properly, and do models for underwriting and pricing produce fair outcomes?

  • Your own data tells you what is happening in your portfolio.
  • Market data tells you whether that differs from what is usual, and therefore where you need to look.
  • Data from the advice process shows what happens before and after your own process: which customers you do not reach, where you drop out of the comparison, and where customers go when they leave.

In this whitepaper you can read, theme by theme, which questions are relevant, which key figures go with them, and how to use market reference data alongside your own figures.

2. Why customer interest now needs to be measurable

Four developments are coming together:

  • Product review. The rules for product approval and review (PARP, under the Wft (Dutch Financial Supervision Act) and the IDD) require you to check periodically whether a product still fits the target market, including with information from distribution.
  • Pricing. The AFM (Dutch Authority for the Financial Markets) has called on insurers to give loyal customers a fair premium, and published points of attention for the personalisation of premiums and terms.
  • AI. The European AI Act classifies AI used for risk assessment and pricing in relation to natural persons in life and health insurance as high-risk. DNB (De Nederlandsche Bank) and the AFM are examining the use of AI in the sector.
  • Climate. Damage from extreme weather is increasing and some risks are becoming harder to insure. This affects both your claims costs and the accessibility of cover.

4. Target market and distribution: does the product fit?

A product review starts with the question of who actually takes out the product in practice, and whether that is the intended target market. Your own data shows who became a customer. Data from the advice process also shows who did not become a customer, and why.

  • Customer profile per insurance product compared with the market: age, household, housing situation, sector and occupational class.
  • Conversion from quote to policy per target group: where are you compared but not chosen?
  • Cancellations shortly after inception: a signal that the product does not fit or has been misunderstood.
  • Channel and intermediary: through which advisers the product reaches which customers.

5. Price and loyalty

The question the AFM asks is simple: do loyal customers pay more than new customers for the same risk, without good reason? Answering it requires careful measurement.

  • Compare the premium change at renewal by policy duration, for policies without a change in cover or insured object.
  • Set this against the premium for new customers with the same profile.
  • Compare the outcome with the market: a difference that exists across the whole market calls for a different explanation than a difference that occurs only at your organisation.
  • Look at behaviour: do long-standing customers leave more often than the market after a premium increase?

Market data on premiums should always be aggregated, after the fact and presented as a range. That way it supports customer interest without sharing competitively sensitive information.

6. Claims: keeping the promise

For the customer, claims handling is the moment of truth. Key figures you will want to compare with the market:

  • Time until a claim is taken into handling, and lead time to settlement
  • Use of a loss adjuster and the effect on lead time
  • Rejections, counter-assessments and complaints per 1,000 claims
  • Use of investigations into the cause of loss or claims investigations and their outcome, always at portfolio level

Claims investigation takes place within the GDPR and the GPO (Code of Conduct for Personal Investigations). A benchmark needs only aggregated counts, never data on individual policyholders or from incident registers.

7. AI and fair outcomes

Models for underwriting, pricing and claims handling can unintentionally treat groups differently, even when protected characteristics are not in the model. A practical approach:

  • Measure the outcomes per target group: rejections, loadings and premiums, for example by age, household and housing situation.
  • Compare with the market: does the difference between groups at your organisation differ from what is usual elsewhere?
  • Treat a deviation as a signal for further investigation of your rules or model, not as proof.
  • Document the testing and the follow-up, so you can show them to the supervisor and auditor.

8. Climate and insurability

Climate-related damage affects both claims costs and customer interest: when risks become uninsurable, customers are left without cover. Useful key figures:

  • Climate-related damage per 1,000 home policies, per region and per month
  • Exclusions, loadings and rejections per region and type of home
  • Share of policies with prevention or sustainability measures
  • Signals of underinsurance, such as a fixed sum insured that has not been reviewed for a long time

The Verbond van Verzekeraars (Dutch Association of Insurers) publishes public data on extreme weather. Market data from the advice process adds the breakdown by portfolio, intermediary and target group.

10. Checklist for your organisation

  • Is the target market documented for each product, and do you periodically measure who actually takes out the product?
  • Do you measure premium development at renewal by policy duration, and can you explain differences?
  • Do you know your claims KPIs, and how they compare with the market?
  • Do you test outcomes of underwriting and pricing models per target group, and document the follow-up?
  • Do you have insight into climate-related damage and insurability per region?
  • Do you use external reference data with a documented methodology, and do you know its limitations?
