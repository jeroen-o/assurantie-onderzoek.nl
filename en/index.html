Research in the sector

Three types of research in the insurance world

Research in the insurance sector serves different purposes: checking a claim, assessing a risk at application, or mapping the sector as a whole. Assurantie-onderzoek.nl provides the market data that fits all three, always aggregated.

1

Claims investigation and investigation into the cause of loss

When there is doubt about how a loss occurred, such as a fire, a collision or a theft. Using interviews, registers, technical investigation and sometimes predictive software, within the GPO (Code of Conduct for Personal Investigations). Read more

2

Underwriting and medical examination

For life and disability insurance, a health declaration or medical examination may be required. Legal limits apply, for example the threshold above which questions about genetic testing are not allowed. We only measure how often acceptance takes place and how long it takes, never medical data.

3

Sector research

DNB examines, among other things, risk management and the use of AI in risk assessment and pricing. The AFM (Dutch Authority for the Financial Markets) publishes research on conduct and duty of care. The Verbond van Verzekeraars carries out sector research through its Data Analytics Centre. We add the view from the intermediary channel.

From behavioural assumptions to actual choices

Insurance law and economics assume rational choices, moral hazard (being less careful because you are insured) and adverse selection (mainly those at high risk take out insurance). Empirical research shows that these assumptions by no means always hold. Rational choice is less dominant than assumed, and underinsurance is common (Faure & Van Boom, Gedragsveronderstellingen en verzekeringen). Our data shows which choices are actually made in the advice process.

  • Which covers, sums insured and excesses do customers really choose, and where does that point to underinsurance?
  • Is it mainly higher-risk customers who take out insurance, or is that not reflected in the claims pattern per segment?
  • What do a default option in the quote or a bundled policy do to the cover customers choose?
  • How often is an in-force policy reviewed mid-term, and what changes then?

Claims & claims investigation

What is an insurance investigation?

In the sector, "assurantieonderzoek" (insurance investigation) also means the investigation that an insurer, or an external agency on the insurer's behalf, carries out in connection with a claim, a payment or an application. The aim is to establish how a loss occurred, to check liability, or to investigate a suspicion of insurance fraud. We make that investigation measurable: how often, why, and with what outcome, against the market.

How a claims procedure works

Notificationvia the intermediary or online
→
File & coverthe claims handler checks the policy
→
Loss adjustingloss adjuster, possibly a counter-assessment
→
Investigationonly if the claim raises questions
→
Settlementwith the customer or repairer

Why an investigation

  • Cause of loss: what happened and who was involved?
  • Cover: is the loss covered by the policy?
  • Liability: who bears the loss?
  • Suspicion of fraud: unusual signals, high amounts or inconsistencies, at application or at the claim

Investigation methods

  • Desk research: public registers such as RDW (vehicle authority), KvK (Chamber of Commerce) and Kadaster (Land Registry), open sources and previous notifications
  • Technical investigation: fire investigation, accident analysis, signs of forced entry, invoice checks
  • Interviews with the policyholder and witnesses
  • Personal investigation, only within strict rules

Rules and privacy

  • The GDPR and the GPO (Code of Conduct for Personal Investigations) of the Verbond van Verzekeraars
  • The insurer's interest is always weighed against the privacy of the person concerned
  • An external investigation agency needs a licence under the Wpbr (Private Security Organisations and Detective Agencies Act)
  • Loss adjusters are registered with NIVRE and follow the code of conduct for loss adjusting organisations

Consequences of established fraud

  • No payment or compensation
  • Recovery of investigation costs and claims paid
  • Termination of the insurance
  • Registration in the incident register and with Stichting CIS
  • Report to the police

What we measure for you

1

Claims benchmark

Your lead time from notification to settlement, the use of loss adjusters, the share of rejections and the number of complaints and counter-assessments, by line of business and against the market.

2

Investigation monitor

How often you start an investigation into the cause of loss or a claims investigation, in which lines of business and at which claim amounts, and how often that leads to a different outcome. Always at portfolio level.

3

Signals at portfolio level

Patterns in repeat claims, notifications shortly after the policy start date and unusual claims frequencies per segment. A tool for your fraud policy, not a judgement on an individual claim.

Scope. Assurantie-onderzoek.nl does not carry out personal investigations into individual policyholders and does not give a judgement on individual claims. We provide aggregated insights into the claims and investigation process. For investigation in an individual file, we refer you to a licensed investigation agency, for example for digital fact-finding in open sources (such as NCRB), or to a loss adjuster.

AI research

AI in the insurance chain: measuring what it does

Insurers and advisers are increasingly using AI: in the advice meeting, underwriting, pricing and claims handling. The AFM and DNB examine this from a supervisory perspective. We measure it from practice: what AI does to speed, quality and outcomes in the advice process, and whether those outcomes are fair for all target groups.

AI monitor in the advice process

  • How often intermediaries use AI, for example for meeting notes and risk assessments
  • What that does to the lead time from quote to policy
  • Whether files with AI support are more complete
  • At which of the intermediaries serving you AI is already common

Fairness benchmark

  • Rejections, loadings and premiums per target group, against the market
  • Signals of undesirable differences between groups
  • Support for your testing of underwriting and pricing models
  • Only aggregated and without protected characteristics such as ethnicity

Predictive signals

  • Cancellation risk per segment and intermediary
  • Signals of underinsurance in the portfolio
  • Forecast of production and claims costs
  • Always with backtesting and a prediction interval

Policy terms as data

  • Policy terms converted with AI into comparable characteristics, with human review
  • Where your terms are better or weaker than the market
  • Which terms determine the adviser's choice in practice
  • A contribution to comparability, for example for cyber insurance

AI usage survey

  • How advisers and insurers use AI, and what they do not use it for
  • What they expect and what concerns them
  • Annually, so that the development becomes visible
  • Reported only by group

Responsible with AI

  • We do not make decisions about individual customers or claims
  • Our own AI applications are described in the methodology
  • Alignment with the points of attention of the AFM and DNB and with the AI Act (see regulation)
AI Act. AI systems for risk assessment and pricing in relation to natural persons for life and health insurance are classified as high-risk under the European AI Act. If you use our insights with such systems, for example to test outcomes per target group, you remain responsible yourself for the requirements the AI Act imposes on your system.

Method & privacy

How the figures are produced

The main points are below. All definitions, calculation methods and limitations are set out in our methodology.

Data source

The figures are based on quotes, applications, policies, policy changes and claim notifications registered via the intermediary channel. They give a picture of that channel and not of the total Dutch insurance market. Direct writers and online providers are outside the scope.

Calculation method

Market share is a weighted share: the number of policies or the premium per party divided by the total in the same period and line of business. For quarters and years it is therefore not the average of individual monthly percentages.

Aggregated only

We only report aggregated figures. Segments with too few observations are not shown, so that figures cannot be traced back to individual consumers, businesses or advisers.

Competition

To avoid restricting competition, we apply fixed safeguards:

  • You see your own position against the market, never figures of individual competitors
  • Comparison groups consist of at least five parties, without any single party determining the outcome
  • Premium information comes only from publicly published sources, with a delay and as a range
  • No information on future or non-public premiums, commissions or underwriting policy
  • Clients do not exchange information with one another through us

Supervision and frameworks

Our methodology is aligned with publications by DNB, AFM, AP (Dutch Data Protection Authority), Kifid, Adfiz, SIVI, NVGA, Contactgroep Automatisering and the Verbond van Verzekeraars.

Classification of lines of business

For personal insurance we follow the ECOICOP v2 classification of CBS: dwelling (121300), private vehicles (121410), travel (121420), health (121200) and other insurance (121900). This allows our premium trends to be compared with the consumer price index. Life and accident insurance (121100) fall outside the CPI and we report them separately, as we do commercial insurance, which we classify by SIVI line of business.
