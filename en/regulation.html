Customer interest & product development

Products that suit the target market, and continue to do so

When developing and reviewing products, insurers must demonstrate that they suit the target market and the distribution channel. Advisers have a duty of care. For both, it is useful to know how the product is chosen in practice, and by whom.

FrameworkWhat is requiredWhat Assurantie-onderzoek.nl provides
Wft (Dutch Financial Supervision Act) and IDD: product approval and review (PARP / POG)Defining a target market, testing the product, recording the distribution strategy and reviewing the product periodically, including on the basis of information from distribution.Who actually takes out the product in practice (customer profile, cover, channel), compared with the intended target market and the market. Plus signals such as high cancellations shortly after inception.
Duty of care of insurer and adviserActing with due care in the customer's interest, including after the policy has been taken out.Insight into renewals, premium development by policy duration and claims handling, as supporting evidence of customer interest.
MGAs: Leidraad Klantbelang Centraal Volmacht (Customer Interest Guideline for MGAs)Agreements between MGA principals and MGAs on customer interest, underwriting and claims handling, with audits. See the Verbond (Dutch Association of Insurers) and the NVGA.A benchmark per MGA: production, cancellations, lead times and rejections, compared with the market.
Kifid (Dutch Financial Services Complaints Institute) and complaints handlingCareful and timely handling of complaints and disputes.Complaints per 1,000 claims, rejections and counter-assessments, compared with the market.
InsurabilityThe Verbond is working on agreements so that as many people and businesses as possible can take out suitable insurance. See insurability.Rejections, loadings and restrictive clauses per target group, and signals of underinsurance.
Conduct supervision

Current AFM themes

The AFM regularly publishes research into the conduct of insurers and advisers. Below are the themes where market data from advice can help. See the AFM's reports and publications for the full texts.

New · October 2026

Customer interest is not the same as customer satisfaction

The AFM (Dutch Authority for the Financial Markets) examined how mid-sized insurers safeguard customer interest in their organisation and decision-making, across three topics: the customer journey, digitalisation and AI, and organisational culture. What makes a customer satisfied is not automatically in their interest: extra questions during underwriting or claims handling may be less appreciated, yet necessary for a careful outcome. The AFM calls on insurers to:

  • explicitly distinguish between customer interest and customer satisfaction, in policy and in practice;
  • monitor whether policy is carried out as intended, and evaluate whether it works. For example: do employees genuinely review AI outputs, does this limit the risks for customers, and does it avoid unnecessary delay?
  • check whether the organisational culture encourages acting in the customer's interest, for example through employee surveys.

What we deliver: outcome indicators alongside satisfaction, such as rejections, loadings, lead times, underinsurance, premium development for loyal customers and complaints; the effect of AI support on lead time and outcome; and employee surveys on customer interest and culture as bespoke research.

Source (in Dutch): AFM, "Make an explicit distinction between customer interest and customer satisfaction" (October 2026)

Premium personalisation and loyal customers

  • The AFM calls for a fair premium for loyal customers and has published points of attention for personalising premiums and terms
  • We provide: premium development at renewal by policy duration, per target group, compared with the market

Active commission transparency

  • The AFM has issued an interpretation of the rules on commission transparency for non-life insurance
  • We provide: trends in advice type and channel, without the commissions of individual parties

Embedded insurance

  • Points of attention for safeguarding customer interest when insurance is sold alongside another product
  • We provide: where recorded, how often customers later review or cancel such insurance through an intermediary

Climate-related damage and insurability

  • Damage caused by climate change is increasingly becoming uninsurable
  • We provide: climate-related damage, exclusions and rejections by region and line of business

AOV: knowledge and experience test

  • The AFM sees room for improvement in the test for income protection insurance for the self-employed (AOV)
  • We provide: which occupational classes, waiting periods and end ages are chosen in advice

Comparability of cyber insurance

  • The AFM draws attention to the comparability of cover
  • We provide: which covers and amounts SME customers choose in practice

Consumer behaviour and AI

  • Research into the use of behavioural insights and AI by banks and insurers, including by DNB (De Nederlandsche Bank) in risk assessment and pricing
  • We provide: an external reference to compare the outcomes of your models per target group with the market

Digitalisation of the market

  • The AFM explored the opportunities and risks of digitalisation for the next ten years
  • We provide: lead times and conversion per channel, and the effect of digital integrations
Please note: we provide analyses of premium development and commission only in aggregated form, after the fact and as a range. In this way they contribute to customer interest without sharing competitively sensitive information.
Prudential supervision & ESG

Climate risk, solvency and sustainability reporting

For non-life insurers, climate change is both an insurance risk and a reporting topic. Market reference data helps to put your own portfolio into perspective.

E

Environmental

Climate-related damage by region and line of business (storm, hail, precipitation, flooding) compared with the market, and the share of policies with prevention or sustainability measures. Supplementing the public extreme weather monitor of the Verbond.

S

Social

Insurability and non-insurance per target group, premium development for loyal customers and the accessibility of cover. Supplementing the Verbond's solidarity monitor and its figures on non-insurance.

G

Governance

Traceable, aggregated data with a documented methodology, usable as an external reference in your risk management, your ORSA and your accountability to supervisors.

FrameworkWhy it is relevant to your portfolioWhat we provide
Solvency II and ORSAIn their own risk and solvency assessment, insurers also assess climate risks, using scenarios.Claims frequency and climate-related damage by region as a reference alongside your own portfolio.
CSRD / ESRSReporting on sustainability, including the impact of climate change. The scope has been revised by the EU; check which obligations apply to you.Reference data on climate-related damage, prevention and sustainability measures in the portfolio.
EU TaxonomyNon-life insurance of climate-related perils can count as a sustainable activity, provided the criteria are met.Insight into which covers for climate-related perils are taken out in the market.
Supervision by DNBDNB examines, among other things, risk management, the use of AI and specific markets such as the AOV.Market reference from the intermediary channel, by line of business and target group.

Claims investigation & fraud

The rules on claims and fraud investigations

An insurer may not simply start an intrusive personal investigation. The insurer's interest must always be weighed against the privacy of the person concerned. Assurantie-onderzoek.nl does not carry out personal investigations itself; we measure the investigation process at portfolio level.

FrameworkWhat it governs
GDPRThe processing of personal data, including when investigating a claim: purpose limitation, necessity and proportionality.
Gedragscode Persoonlijk Onderzoek (GPO, Code of Conduct for Personal Investigations)The rules of the Verbond van Verzekeraars for personal investigations, such as observation and open-source research, and the rights of the person concerned.
Incident registers and Stichting CISWhere fraud has been established, this may be followed by registration in the insurer's incident register and in the warning system at Stichting CIS, in accordance with the protocol for financial institutions.
Wpbr (Private Security Organisations and Detective Agencies Act)An external private investigation agency requires a licence and follows a privacy code of conduct.
Loss adjustingLoss adjusters are registered with NIVRE and follow the Code of Conduct for loss adjusting organisations.
Underwriting and medical dataFor life and disability insurance, statutory limits apply, such as the question limit for genetic testing. We never process medical data.

Why an investigation

  • Establishing the cause of loss and cover
  • Verifying liability
  • Suspicion of fraud at application or claim

Methods

  • Desk research in registers and open sources
  • Technical investigation
  • Interviews with the insured and witnesses
  • Personal investigation within the GPO

Consequences of fraud

  • No payment
  • Recovery of costs and payments
  • Termination of the insurance
  • Registration and, where applicable, a report to the police

What we provide

  • How often you use investigations, by line of business, compared with the market
  • How often this leads to a different outcome
  • Signals at portfolio level, never per person

Competition

Market intelligence without competitively sensitive details

Benchmarks are valuable, but must not restrict competition. That is why fixed safeguards apply, in line with the rules of the ACM (Netherlands Authority for Consumers and Markets) on information exchange.

  • You see your own position compared with the market, never figures of individual competitors
  • Peer groups consist of at least five parties, without any single party determining the outcome
  • Premium information only from public sources, with a delay and as a range
  • No information on future or non-public premiums, commissions or underwriting policy
  • Clients do not exchange information with one another through us
