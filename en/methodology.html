1. Purpose and scope

Assurantie-onderzoek.nl provides market intelligence to non-life insurers, life and income protection insurers, MGAs (managing general agents) and insurance intermediaries. The reports show the client's position compared with the market or with an anonymous peer group. They cover personal and commercial insurance that is advised on, taken out, administered and, in the event of a claim, handled through the intermediary channel.

2. Data source

  • Source: the Blinqx advice, comparison, administration and CRM software used by insurance intermediaries and MGAs.
  • Units: quotes (premium comparisons), applications, policies, policy changes, renewals, cancellations and claim notifications, as recorded in these systems.
  • Message traffic: where available, standardised messages in accordance with the SIVI standard.
  • Coverage: determined per study. Each report states the number of intermediaries, MGAs, quotes, policies and claim notifications in the period studied, together with the reference date.
  • Premium data: publicly available premiums for fixed model profiles, recorded with source and date.
  • Supplementary open data: where indicated, publications from sources including CBS (Statistics Netherlands), DNB (De Nederlandsche Bank) and the Verbond van Verzekeraars (Dutch Association of Insurers; Data Analytics Centre).

The figures relate to the Blinqx network and the intermediary channel. They are not necessarily representative of the Dutch insurance market as a whole. Direct writers, online providers and intermediaries using other software are not included.

3. Classification of lines of business

We classify personal insurance according to the ECOICOP v2 classification of CBS, so that premium trends can be compared with the consumer price index. Commercial insurance and income protection insurance are classified by SIVI line of business.

4. Definitions

TermDefinition
QuoteA premium comparison in which an insurer appears. Multiple comparisons within one file for the same insurer and line of business in the same month count as one.
ApplicationAn application sent from the file to an insurer or MGA
New policyA policy with a recorded inception date in the period that is not a continuation of an existing policy with the same insurer
Policy changeA change to an in-force policy, such as different cover, a different sum insured or a different insured object
RenewalThe extension of a policy on the contract expiry date
CancellationA terminated policy, regardless of the reason. A switch to another insurer through the same intermediary counts as a cancellation at the former insurer.
Cancellation rateThe number of cancellations in the period divided by the number of in-force policies at the start of the period, annualised
Market shareThe number of new policies (or the new premium, where so stated) at the insurer divided by the total in the same period and line of business. This is a weighted share, not the average of individual monthly percentages.
ConversionThe number of applications divided by the number of quotes in which the insurer appeared
IndexThe share at an intermediary, in a region or in a segment, divided by the national share, multiplied by 100
PremiumThe annual premium including insurance premium tax and excluding policy fees, unless stated otherwise
Peer groupAn anonymous group of at least 5 comparable providers, in which no single party accounts for more than 25% of the volume

5. Calculation methods

Quartiles and ranges

The position within a peer group is expressed in quartiles. For premiums, we show for fixed model profiles the lowest and highest premium, the 25th and 75th percentiles and the median, never the premium of an individual competitor.

Indicative reason for losing out

For quotes in which an insurer appeared but which did not lead to an application with that insurer, we compare premium, cover, excess and underwriting outcome with those of the insurer that was chosen. The largest difference is taken as the indicative reason. This is an inference, not a measured reason. Which insurer received the application is not reported.

Premium development at renewal

The average percentage change in premium at renewal, for policies without a change in cover or insured object in the preceding twelve months, broken down by policy duration. This ensures that the change is not the result of different cover.

Signals of underinsurance

The share of policies whose sum insured was not determined using a home contents value calculator or rebuild value calculator, or has not been reviewed for more than five years. This is a signal, not a determination of underinsurance.

Forecast

The forecast of your own production is based on the historical relationship between quotes, applications and new policies, with seasonal adjustment. The range shows the 80% prediction interval. The method is tested against actual outcomes every quarter.

Message traffic (Aplaza and ADN)

We count standardised messages exchanged between intermediaries, MGAs and insurers via Aplaza and as ADN messages (SIVI standard): application, policy, policy change, renewal, cancellation and claim. Repeated and duplicate messages for the same policy and the same event are de-duplicated. A message is not a policy: one policy can generate several messages in a year. We therefore report message volumes as an index or as a share by message type, not as a number of policies. Only intermediaries and MGAs with a message connection are visible. We use the number and type of messages, not their content about individuals.

6. Claims and claims investigation

TermDefinition
Claim notificationA claim recorded in the file through the intermediary or the MGA
Taken into handlingThe date on which the claim file was created and cover was checked
Lead timeThe number of working days from notification to the final payment or the rejection, median
Use of loss adjustingA claim for which a loss adjuster was engaged, as recorded in the file
InvestigationAn investigation into the cause of loss, a technical investigation or an external investigation recorded in the claim file, regardless of the outcome
Climate-related damageDamage caused by storm, hail, precipitation, flooding or lightning, per 1,000 in-force home policies
SignalA pattern at portfolio level that deviates from the market, such as a claim shortly after inception or repeat claims. Never a judgement on an individual claim.

For investigations, we count only the fact and the outcome category (no deviation, payment adjusted, rejected). We do not use data from incident registers or the warning system of Stichting CIS, nor the content of investigation reports.

6a. Use of AI in our analyses

  • Policy terms as data: we use language models to convert public policy terms and conditions into comparable characteristics. Every output is checked by a researcher before it is included in a report.
  • Classification: free text, such as a cancellation reason or cause of loss, may be classified into fixed categories using AI. The share classified in this way is stated in the report.
  • Forecasts and signals: statistical models on aggregated data, with backtesting and a prediction interval.
  • Limits: we do not use AI to make decisions about individual customers, policies or claims, and we do not use protected characteristics such as ethnicity. Personal data is not provided to external AI services.
  • Fairness analyses: we report differences between target groups as a signal for further investigation, not as proof of discrimination.

7. Data quality

  • Deduplication: duplicate quotes, applications and policies within one file are merged.
  • Plausibility: values outside realistic limits are excluded, for example a negative premium or a lead time of more than three years.
  • Missing values: these are reported separately as "unknown" and are not imputed, unless explicitly stated.
  • Consistency: every month we check the reconciliation between quotes, applications and policies, and between policies and claim notifications.
  • Revision: late registrations may still change earlier months. Figures are final 2 months after the end of the period. Before then, they are marked as provisional.

8. Privacy

  • Reports contain only aggregated data, without personal data of consumers, business owners or advisers.
  • Segments with fewer than 25 observations are not shown.
  • The intermediary or the MGA is the controller for the customer file. The use of file data for aggregated market research will be laid down in an agreement with each participating party (in preparation). This will set out the purpose, pseudonymisation prior to analysis, the retention periods and the prohibition on re-identification.
  • The intended legal basis is legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). The balancing test and the DPIA to support this are in preparation. We will not process customer data for research before these have been completed.
  • We do not use special categories of personal data, such as health data, or personal data relating to criminal convictions and offences.
  • Underlying data is processed in pseudonymised form and anonymised or deleted after 5 years.
  • Age, income, sum insured and postcode are reported only in bands.

9. Competition

  • A client sees only its own position compared with the market or an anonymous peer group.
  • No figures on individual competitors are provided.
  • Premium information comes only from public sources and is shown as a range, with a delay of at least 4 weeks.
  • No information is provided on future or non-public premiums, commissions or underwriting policy.
  • Clients do not exchange information with one another through Assurantie-onderzoek.nl.
  • Clients do not use the insights for loyalty penalties, margin personalisation or steering advisers (set out in the licence terms).

10. Limitations

  • Coverage is limited to the Blinqx network. Direct channels and other software are not included.
  • Quality depends on what intermediaries and MGAs record, for example the cancellation reason, the cause of loss and the use of a loss adjuster.
  • The reason for losing out, the signals and the forecast are inferences subject to uncertainty.
  • Claims reported directly to the insurer, without the involvement of the intermediary, are not always visible.
